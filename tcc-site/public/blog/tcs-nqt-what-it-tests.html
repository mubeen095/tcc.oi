What the TCS NQT actually tests. And what it does not.

Most NQT advice is a list of topics. That is not the problem. The problem is that the paper is timed in a way that punishes a particular habit, and almost nobody explains which one.

The Coding Company 10 November 2026 10 min read

The TCS NQT is not a difficult paper. It is a fast one. Almost everyone who fails it can solve the questions given enough time — the paper's whole design is to find out what happens when the time is not enough. That single fact should reorganise how you prepare, and for most candidates it does not.

What follows is the structure of the paper, the five skills it is actually measuring underneath the topic list, and the four habits that cost people marks they could otherwise have kept. Confirm the current pattern on TCS's own portal before your test date: section counts move between cycles, and a guide written last year is not a substitute for the notification you were sent.

The shape of the paper

The NQT is a single timed sitting split into sections, each with its own clock. When a section's time ends it moves on, and you cannot return to it. That last sentence is the most important operational fact about the exam, and it is the one candidates discover too late.

  • Numerical ability. Percentages, ratios, averages, time–speed–distance, profit and loss, simple and compound interest, data sufficiency, and chart reading. The arithmetic is school-level. The constraints are speed and reading accuracy.
  • Verbal ability. Reading comprehension, sentence completion, error detection, para-jumbles and vocabulary in context. This section is where strong English speakers bank time for the rest of the paper.
  • Reasoning ability. Series, coding-decoding, blood relations, direction sense, syllogisms, seating arrangements and figure series. Largely pattern recognition under time pressure.
  • Advanced section. Coding and, depending on the role you applied for, additional technical or domain questions. For many profiles this section is where the offer level is effectively decided.
The structural trap

Because sections are locked, a single question you refuse to abandon can cost you an entire later section. The paper does not reward persistence. It rewards triage. Candidates who do well are not usually the ones who solve the hardest question — they are the ones who never spend four minutes on it.

What it is really measuring

Strip away the topic labels and the paper is testing five things. Two of them are not on the syllabus at all.

01
Arithmetic fluency

Can you compute a percentage change without reaching for a pen? Roughly half of numerical ability is decided by whether basic operations are automatic.

02
Reading precision

A large share of wrong answers are right calculations applied to the wrong quantity. The paper tests whether you noticed "excluding tax" and "per annum".

03
Triage under pressure

Not on the syllabus, and worth more than any single topic. Knowing which question to skip is a skill you can practise deliberately.

04
Composure after a bad section

Also not on the syllabus. Candidates who carry a ruined section into the next one lose two sections, not one.

05
Code that runs

In the advanced section, a working brute-force solution beats an elegant incomplete one. The evaluator runs your code; it does not read your intent.

The four places marks actually disappear

These four account for most of the gap between a candidate's practice scores and their real score.

Spending more than 90 seconds on one question

The maths is that a three-minute question you eventually solve costs you four one-minute questions you never see. Set a hard ceiling per question and hold it.

The fix — a written ceiling

Before the test, decide the number. Ninety seconds for numerical, sixty for reasoning. Write it on the rough sheet. A rule you wrote down is easier to obey than one you intended.

Reading the passage in the wrong order

In comprehension, reading the questions first tells you which paragraph matters. Candidates who read the full passage first routinely run out of time on the last two questions.

The fix — question-first reading

Skim the questions, then read. You are not being tested on enjoying the passage; you are being tested on locating a specific claim.

Untested mental arithmetic

People who have not done a percentage change without a calculator since school lose 20–30 seconds per numerical question, which compounds into an entire section.

The fix — twenty minutes a day

Drill the eight operations that appear constantly: percentage change, ratio simplification, average with replacement, work rate, simple interest, speed conversion, and fraction-to-decimal recall.

Coding without a local test

Submitting code you have not run, on an interface you have not used, with input parsing you have not checked, is the most avoidable failure in the advanced section.

The fix — dry-run the interface

Practise in the same editor or on the same platform at least twice before the real thing. Read input until EOF; do not assume a fixed number of lines.

Three weeks, honestly

Most candidates over-prepare topics and under-prepare timing. This split corrects that.

  • Week one — mechanics. One topic a day, twenty questions each, no timer. The goal is accuracy, not speed. If you cannot get eight of ten right untimed, timing is irrelevant.
  • Week two — sections under clock. Full sections, real duration, no pauses. Log the questions you skipped and the ones that ate time. The log is more useful than the score.
  • Week three — full sittings. Two complete papers in exam order, at the same time of day as your slot, on a laptop with the same keyboard. Fatigue is part of the test and it should not be new on the day.

"The NQT does not ask whether you can solve it. It asks whether you can decide, in eight seconds, that you should not."

What the NQT does not test

It is worth being clear about this, because candidates routinely over-invest in things the paper ignores.

  • It does not test whether you can build software. The advanced section tests whether you can solve a defined problem in code under time.
  • It does not test your degree, your college, or your CGPA. Those filters happen elsewhere.
  • It does not test depth in any single subject. Breadth and speed beat depth here — which is also why a good NQT score is not evidence you will be good at the job, and a bad one is not evidence you will be bad at it.

That last point matters for how you interpret your own result. A test that rewards triage is measuring a real workplace skill, but it is not measuring the whole of you. Treat the score as diagnostic information about a specific constraint — time pressure — rather than a verdict.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the TCS NQT?

The sitting runs around 90 minutes in total, split into separately timed sections. Section counts and durations have changed between cycles, so confirm the current pattern in the notification you received rather than relying on a guide.

Is there negative marking in the TCS NQT?

There is no negative marking in the sections most candidates sit, which means an unanswered question scores exactly the same as a wrong one. Under that rule, guessing is rational — but only after you have secured the questions you can actually solve.

Can I go back to a previous section?

No. Each section is locked when its timer ends. This is the single most important operational fact about the paper and the reason triage matters more than raw ability.

How many questions do I need to get right?

There is no published cut-off that applies to everyone, because the bar moves with the role, the hiring location and the candidate pool. Aim to finish every section with time left rather than to hit a specific count.

Does the NQT score expire?

TCS uses NQT scores for its own hiring windows, and the validity depends on the cycle you sat. Check the current notification for how long your score is considered.
