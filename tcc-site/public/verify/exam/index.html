Verification · Assessment

The assessment, exactly as it runs.

Format, timings and marking are fixed before a cohort opens and are not changed mid-cohort. This is the whole specification — there is no second document that says something different.

Format by level

Each CERT+ credential ends in one assessment. The level determines how much of it is machine-scored and how much is read by a person.

LevelDurationWhat you submitMarked by
Foundation40 min25 objective items plus one short written explanationAuto, then a reviewer reads the written part
Practitioner60 min20 objective items plus one role artifact built in the exam windowAuto plus one reviewer
Strategist75 minA written case response plus a recorded defence of your trade-offsTwo reviewers, independently
Executive45 minA decision memo addressed to a named stakeholderReviewer panel

Marking rules

  • No negative marking. An unanswered question scores zero, never less. Guessing is rational and we do not punish it.
  • Objective items are machine-scored. One correct option, no partial credit, no manual override.
  • Artifacts are scored against the published rubric on the marking page, by a reviewer who never sees your name.
  • Pass is 60 of 100 overall, with no criterion below 40. A strong artifact cannot carry a collapse in judgement.
  • Results within five working days where a person marks your work; immediately where the assessment is entirely objective.
  • One retake within 30 days, included. The higher score stands. We publish both attempts to you, not just the better one.

AI tools during the assessment

We teach people to work with AI, so banning it in the exam would test the wrong skill. Instead:

  • You may use AI tools on Practitioner, Strategist and Executive assessments.
  • You must disclose which tools you used and for what, in a required field.
  • You are marked on the quality of your verification, not the speed of your generation. An unverified model output that looks confident scores badly, and the rubric says so explicitly.
  • Foundation assessments are closed-book, because the point of that level is knowing what the tool is doing.

What invalidates a result

  • An artifact you cannot explain when asked about it, in a short follow-up call.
  • Undisclosed AI use where the rubric requires disclosure.
  • More than one person on the same submission.

Invalidation is not an accusation of cheating. It means the result cannot be defended to an employer, and a credential that cannot be defended is worth nothing to you.

What we change, and what we never change

Question content is refreshed between cohorts. That is normal and healthy — questions leak, and a leaked paper measures memory instead of ability.

What does not change mid-cohort: the number of questions, the time limit, the rubric, the pass mark, or the marking criteria. If any of those must change, it applies to the next cohort and it is announced here with a date.
