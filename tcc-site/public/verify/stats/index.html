Verification · Numbers

The numbers we publish, and the ones we refuse to.

Every education company eventually publishes a percentage. Most of them are true and useless — a numerator without a denominator, a window chosen after the fact. Here is our publication rule, fixed in advance.

Where we stand today

No cohort has completed a full cycle

The first CERT+ cohort has not yet finished assessment, so there is no pass rate to report and no placement data to aggregate. This page will stay in this state until there is. We would rather show you an empty table with a rule attached than a number with no denominator.

The publication rule

Each metric below has a fixed definition and a fixed publication trigger. The definition is written before the data exists, which is the only way to stop a definition quietly changing to suit a result.

MetricPublished whenDefinition
Assessment pass rateFirst cohort completesPasses ÷ candidates who sat the final assessment. Withdrawals before the assessment are reported separately, never folded in or out.
Score distributionFirst cohort completesEvery score, in bands of ten, including the failures. No curve, no rescaling.
Completion rateFirst cohort completesEnrolled who finished ÷ enrolled who started, with the start date defined as the first live session attended.
Placement rateMinimum 30 people through the placement trackVerified offer letters ÷ people who completed the placement track and actively searched. Window: 180 days from track completion.
Median packageSame thresholdMedian of verified accepted offers, in INR LPA, fixed component. Not the mean, and never the maximum as a headline.
Time to offerSame thresholdMedian days from track completion to written offer.

Why 30

Below thirty people, a median is a rumour. One outlier moves it by several lakh, and the person reading it cannot tell whether they are looking at a programme or at one unusually good month. Thirty is not a statistical guarantee; it is the point at which a number stops being anecdote.

What we will never publish

  • Highest package as a headline. One offer is not an outcome. If we report a maximum at all, it appears beside the median and the count.
  • A percentage without its denominator. "94% placement" means nothing without knowing who was counted, who was excluded, and over what window.
  • Numbers from a window chosen after the result. The window is fixed in the table above before the data exists.
  • Salary figures for roles nobody in the cohort took. Market-rate estimates are fine as context, but they are labelled as estimates and never mixed into outcome figures.
  • Aggregates that include people who did not search. Someone who took a family business job was not placed and was not failed. Mixing the two flatters the number.

Corrections

If a published figure turns out to be wrong, it is corrected here with the old value, the new value and the date, rather than quietly edited. A correction history is more useful to you than a clean page.

Write to hello@thecodingcompany.io if you believe a number here is wrong. You will get a reply from a person, and if you are right, the correction goes on this page.
