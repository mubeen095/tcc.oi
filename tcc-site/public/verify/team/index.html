Who writes the papers.

You are not looking at headshots. That is deliberate, and this page explains why — along with the process behind every question you sit.

The rule about people

A name appears on this site only with a link you can check. A photograph appears only if it is the person. No stock photography, no invented biographies, no "industry expert" with no industry.

Course sites are full of instructors who exist only as a headshot and a first name. We would rather publish fewer people and let you verify each one, because a biography you cannot check is decoration.

How a question gets into a paper

  1. Drafted from a real round. Every item starts from a documented hiring or role task, not from a textbook chapter. The source is recorded in the item file.
  2. Answered cold by a second person. If the second person cannot reach the intended answer without hints, the item is rewritten or dropped. Ambiguity in a test is a defect, not a difficulty setting.
  3. Checked against the rubric. Each item must name the criterion it is measuring. An item that measures nothing specific is cut, however clever it looks.
  4. Sit as a candidate. Someone who has not seen the item takes it under the real time limit. If it cannot be finished in the allotted time by a competent person, the timing is wrong or the item is too long.
  5. Reviewed after every cohort. Items where strong candidates cluster on one wrong option are treated as item faults first, candidate faults second.

What the reviewer sees

Reviewers mark blind against a candidate ID. They see your artifact, your disclosure of AI use, and the rubric. They do not see your name, your college, your city, or what you paid — because all four change how people mark, and not in a direction that helps you.

Named instructors, published before each cohort

Before a cohort opens, this page lists the people teaching it: name, what they have shipped, and a link to verify it. If that list is not here, the cohort is not open, and any page suggesting otherwise is out of date.

This is a self-imposed constraint. It is easy to write a page of impressive-sounding instructors in an afternoon. It is hard to be accountable for one, which is exactly why the rule exists.

Corrections and complaints

If something you were taught was wrong, write to hello@thecodingcompany.io. Corrections are logged, and where a taught error affected an assessment, affected candidates are re-marked at no cost.
