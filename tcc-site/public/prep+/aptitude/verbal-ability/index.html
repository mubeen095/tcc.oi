5 questions with full solutions

The correct option is highlighted. Read the explanation even when you get it right — the method is the point.

01Choose the correct sentence.
  1. A.Neither of the two candidates were present.
  2. B.Neither of the two candidates was present.
  3. C.Neither of the two candidate were present.
  4. D.Neither of the two candidates are present.
Solution. "Neither" is singular, so it takes the singular verb "was". The phrase "of the two candidates" does not change the subject.
02Select the antonym of "candid".
  1. A.Frank
  2. B.Evasive
  3. C.Honest
  4. D.Blunt
Solution. "Candid" means open and straightforward. Its antonym is "evasive" — the others are synonyms.
03Identify the error: "The number of students in the class are increasing every year."
  1. A.The number of students
  2. B.in the class
  3. C.are increasing
  4. D.every year
Solution. "The number of" is singular and demands "is increasing". Contrast with "a number of students are", where the plural verb is correct.
04Fill in the blank: "He was so ______ in his work that he forgot to eat."
  1. A.engrossed
  2. B.engaging
  3. C.engrossing
  4. D.engaged
Solution. The correct idiom is "engrossed in". "Engaged in" also works grammatically but "engrossed" carries the sense of total absorption the sentence needs.
05Rearrange into a coherent sentence: (P) the experiment (Q) despite the setback (R) was repeated (S) with renewed precision
  1. A.PRQS
  2. B.QRPS
  3. C.RQPS
  4. D.QPRS
Solution. "Despite the setback" is a subordinate opener, then the subject "the experiment", then "was repeated", then the manner. That gives QPRS.
Solution answers are worked values. Where a decimal result is not one of the options, the closest listed option is marked correct; the explanation gives the exact figure.
